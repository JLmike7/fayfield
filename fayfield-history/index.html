Fayfield History

Before the lawns, this was an airfield.

What follows is what public sources currently support, with the uncertainties left in. We aren’t hosting old photographs here, and we aren’t reprinting other people’s documents. No ordinary residents are pictured.

What public sources currently support

  • A 2019 YorksPast post by Stephen H. Smith says Fayfield was developed in Springettsbury Township on the site of York County’s first commercial airport. Smith dates that airport to 1930–1937 along Haines Road (then also called Airport Road), between what are now 7th and 9th Avenues.

  • Smith writes that the full Fayfield subdivision was approved in 1947 and managed by Morgan, Zeigler and Philips, Inc., and that a May 24, 1947 advertisement in The Gazette and Daily announced the founding. We have not independently retrieved that 1947 advertisement.

  • Smith reports that by April 9, 1949, The Gazette and Daily said 173 of 415 lots were sold and 34 homes were built, and that most lots were built on by the mid-1950s except where planning for Interstate 83 limited the west side. I-83 opening in 1959 is given in that same post.

  • A 2019 York Daily Record history piece by Jim McClure quotes racially restrictive occupancy language from the 1947 Fayfield subdivision plan. Those restrictions are not legal today. We are not reprinting the clause here; the newspaper article is the public source.

Uncertainty

The name “Fayfield” may come from “Fay,” a relative of a developer. Smith labels that speculation, not a confirmed origin.

This isn’t a complete timeline. A fuller history needs rights-cleared documents from archives.
